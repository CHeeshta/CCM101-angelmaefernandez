# Reflection

After completing this laboratory, I realized that choosing a cloud platform is not just about picking the biggest or most popular provider. I was most impressed by **Microsoft Azure** because of how well it connects with other Microsoft products. Since many organizations already use Windows Server, Microsoft 365, and Active Directory, Azure can make moving some services to the cloud easier.

I also noticed that **AWS, Azure, and GCP have many similarities**. All three platforms provide services for virtual machines, storage, databases, networking, identity management, and Kubernetes. They also have management consoles where users can create and manage their cloud resources. The main difference is how each provider organizes its services and where each platform is strongest.

Business requirements are important when selecting a cloud provider. A company with a limited budget may have different priorities from a large enterprise. Existing technologies also matter. For example, a university that already uses Microsoft products may benefit more from Azure, while a company focused on artificial intelligence or cloud-native applications may consider GCP. This showed me that the best cloud platform depends on the actual needs of the client.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me to look at the requirements first before making a recommendation. I learned that there is no single cloud provider that is perfect for every situation. The decision should be based on factors such as cost, scalability, existing infrastructure, services, and future plans.

My GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio also improved during this mission. I added research files, comparison tables, recommendations, and screenshots to document my work. I also became more comfortable using Markdown and organizing technical information. Overall, this activity helped me understand cloud platforms better and made my portfolio more organized and useful.
